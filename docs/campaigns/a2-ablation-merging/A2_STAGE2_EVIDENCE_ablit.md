# Stage-2 Evidence Review: Refusal-Direction Ablation for A2 (uTC / W1 / W2)

**Scope.** Literature grounding for the abliteration leg of the A2 "concise-uncensored Qwen3.6-27B" plan: deriving a refusal direction `r-hat` from the aligned BASE, orthogonalizing it out of TC's residual-writing matrices to get `uTC`, using `uTC` as the merge carrier (`W1`), and a "hypertrophy governor" (`W2 = W1 - mu*CA`) that re-injects a controlled dose of alignment. Confidence tags: **[ESTABLISHED]** = peer-reviewed / widely reproduced; **[EMERGING]** = arXiv preprint, plausible methodology, not yet widely reproduced; **[LOW-CONFIDENCE]** = single-author/unreviewed preprint or community blog post — treat as a hypothesis pointer, not a citation to lean on.

Note on sourcing: several arXiv PDFs could not be parsed to full text by the fetch tool (binary/FlateDecode streams); where that happened I fell back to the HTML mirror or to search-engine synthesis and flag the specific numbers pulled that way as **[secondary-synthesis, not directly verified against primary text]**.

## 1. The core method — Arditi et al. 2024, arXiv:2406.11717

**"Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction"** — Arditi, Obeso, Syed, Paleka, Panickssery/Rimsky, Gurnee, Nanda. **[ESTABLISHED]** — this is the paper the entire "abliteration" cottage industry (mlabonne's blog, Heretic, FailSpy's abliterator, etc.) descends from, and it is peer-reproduced across many open-weight families.

**Difference-of-means direction.** For layer `l` and a chosen token position (post-instruction template tokens, i.e. the last few tokens before generation starts), compute
`r_i^(l) = mu_i^(l) - nu_i^(l)`
where `mu^(l)` is the mean residual-stream activation over harmful instructions and `nu^(l)` the mean over harmless instructions, both at layer `l`. This is done for **every layer and every candidate token position**, producing a bank of candidate directions, not one direction — direction *selection* is a separate step (below). Sign convention: the vector points from "harmless" toward "harmful," so subtracting its projection removes what pushes the model toward refusal.

**Datasets.** Train set: **128 harmful instructions** (pooled from AdvBench, MaliciousInstruct, TDC2023 red-teaming set) + **128 harmless instructions** (Alpaca). Validation set: **32 harmful** (HarmBench) + **32 harmless** (Alpaca), kept disjoint from the train pool and from the harm-benchmark used for final evaluation. This is a useful anchor for our own prompt-set sizing — Arditi et al. found ~128/128 sufficient at 7B–70B scale; they did not need thousands of examples.

**Layer/direction selection.** Not "pick the layer with the largest mean gap" — they score every candidate `r^(l)` on the validation set and select `r*` by: (a) minimizing a **bypass_score** (residual refusal rate on harmful validation prompts when the direction is ablated), (b) requiring **induce_score > 0** (the direction, when *added*, must be able to induce refusal on harmless prompts — a sanity check that it's really the refusal-relevant direction and not noise), (c) **KL divergence < 0.1** between ablated and original next-token distributions on harmless prompts (a coherence/side-effect guard), and (d) a **layer-position constraint `l < 0.8*L`**, explicitly excluding late layers close to unembedding, which tend to encode output-formatting/lexical features rather than the semantic refusal decision. This last constraint is directly relevant to us: for a 27B-class model, do not source `r-hat` from the last ~20% of layers.

**Weight orthogonalization vs. inference-time ablation.** Inference-time ablation subtracts the projection of the direction at every layer/position during the forward pass: `x' = x - r-hat (r-hat^T x)`. Weight orthogonalization bakes the same operation into the weights so no runtime hook is needed:
`W_out' = W_out - r-hat r-hat^T W_out`
applied to **every matrix that writes into the residual stream** — token embedding, positional embedding, attention output projections (`attn.o_proj`), and MLP output/down projections (`mlp.down_proj`), plus matching bias terms. The paper proves in an appendix that this is mathematically **exactly equivalent** to the inference-time hook (same forward-pass outputs), so weight-orthogonalization is not an approximation — it's a compile-time version of the same intervention. This validates our plan's targeting of `attn.o_proj + mlp.down_proj` specifically (these two are the standard, load-bearing targets; embedding/pos-embedding matter less in modern RoPE-based decoder-only models and are commonly skipped in practice, which is consistent with what community tools like Heretic do — see §4).

**Capability retention.** On the models tested, MMLU moved within **±1.5%** (mostly under 1%) and GSM8K within **±1.5%**; TruthfulQA showed a more consistent **2–3 point drop** (removing the refusal direction costs some hedging/truthfulness behavior specifically, not general capability — worth watching if your downstream eval includes anything truthfulness-flavored). Two outlier models (Qwen-7B and Yi-34B, in the original 13-model sweep) fell outside the 99% CI band the rest of the models stayed within, i.e. **some model families are measurably more fragile to this exact intervention than others** — and notably one of the two named outliers is a Qwen model, which is a direct amber flag for our Qwen3.6-27B target (see §4/§6).

**Cross-checkpoint transfer.** The paper does **not** test whether a direction extracted from one checkpoint (e.g. base) transfers to a different fine-tune of the same base. All of Arditi et al.'s experiments derive the direction from and apply it to the *same* model. This is the central gap our plan needs outside evidence for — covered in §5.

## 2. Failure modes and risks

**2a. Refusal is not always a clean single direction through the origin — "Refusal in LLMs is an Affine Function," Marshall, Scherlis, Belrose, arXiv:2411.09003. [EMERGING].**
Core argument: linear-direction ablation implicitly assumes the origin (zero activation) is a neutral "no behavior" point, but for many behaviors — refusal included — the typical/default activation level is *not* zero. Pure directional ablation can therefore be "an undesirably strong intervention": the paper reports that on at least one model (Hermes/Eagle RWKV v5) plain directional ablation produced **incoherent, ungrammatical output** — not just "less refusal," but broken generation. Their fix, Affine Concept Editing (ACE), adds back a reference-point projection (an offset term fit from negative/harmless examples) alongside the direction-removal and scaled-steering terms, landing the edited activation in a plausible region of activation space instead of near-zero. **Practical takeaway for us**: pure orthogonal projection (`x - r r^T x`, what our plan does) is the same class of intervention the paper flags as risky; the failure mode to watch for is degraded *coherence*, not just under/over-refusal, and it is model-family-dependent (severe on RWKV, milder on the Llama-family models the original Arditi paper used).

**2b. Refusal is not strictly one-dimensional — several converging 2025–2026 results. [EMERGING/mixed rigor].**
- **"The Geometry of Refusal in Large Language Models: Concept Cones and Representational Independence,"** Wollschläger, Elstner, Geisler, Cohen-Addad, Günnemann, Gasteiger, arXiv:2502.17420. Proposes refusal lives in a multi-dimensional **polyhedral cone** of directions rather than a single ray, and examines when refusal is/isn't representationally independent from other (useful) features — i.e., direction-based ablation can, in principle, clip capability-relevant components that are entangled with the cone.
- **"There Is More to Refusal in Large Language Models than a Single Direction,"** Joad, Hawasly, Boughorbel, Durrani, Sencar (QCRI/HBKU), arXiv:2602.02132. Across 11 refusal *categories* (safety refusal, incomplete-request refusal, anthropomorphization refusal, over-refusal, etc.) on Gemma-2-9B-IT and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, they find geometrically distinct directions per category, but — importantly — steering along *any* of them has *similar refusal efficacy*, i.e. the categories share a common behavioral "knob" even though their geometric encodings differ. This is a softer version of the multi-direction claim than it first sounds: it argues for multiple *encodings* of a similar *control signal*, not necessarily multiple independent mechanisms that all need separate ablation.
- **"Over-Refusal and Representation Subspaces: A Mechanistic Analysis of Task-Conditioned Refusal in Aligned LLMs,"** Maskey, Dras, Naseem, arXiv:2603.27518. Finds refusal is represented via **task-conditioned subspaces** rather than one global vector — different harmful-request categories can route through different subspaces. Direct implication: a single global `r-hat`, however well-selected, may under-ablate specific request categories that route through a subspace it doesn't cover well. This bears on our 28-prompt refusal probe's *coverage*: a single discriminating meta-prompt tier is a reasonable pilot signal but is unlikely to certify uniform ablation across all refusal categories.
- **AAAI "SOM Directions Are Better than One: Multi-Directional Refusal Suppression"** — title and venue (AAAI, peer-reviewed) are confirmed, but the fetch tool could not extract full text (binary PDF); treat as **corroborating-by-title-only** for now — it argues multiple simultaneous suppression directions outperform a single one, consistent with the cone/subspace literature above, but I could not verify its specific method or numbers first-hand.

**2c. Direct evidence bearing on "hypertrophy" (over-driving an already-decensored model). [LOW-CONFIDENCE — flag clearly].**
I found no controlled study that ablates a model that is *already* fine-tuned to be less-aligned (our TC→uTC step, or worse, stacking uTC with Fable's own de-alignment) and measures whether the refusal-direction magnitude/effect "overshoots" into degenerate territory. The closest analogues:
- **"Abliteration Is Not a Scalpel: Off-Target Effects of Refusal Removal on Decision Disposition Across Model Families,"** Fafuła, arXiv:2607.17427v1 (single author). **[LOW-CONFIDENCE]**. Finds that even when refusal-narrow metrics (JSON validity, instruction-following) stay intact post-ablation, ablated models show measurable **personality/disposition drift**: +7–12pp more "optimistic" risk-taking on identical evidence, more verbose self-justification, thinner uncertainty-hedging language, and *model-dependent* confidence shifts (Gemma-ablit got less confident, Qwen-ablit got *more* confident, non-overlapping CIs). The Qwen-specific finding (ablation → overconfidence) is the closest thing in the literature to a documented "over-drive" side-effect on a Qwen model, and is germane to your `mu` sweep: it's evidence that pure `W1` (mu=0) could plausibly manifest as *overconfident under-hedged* creative output even if refusal-probe verdicts look clean, which is exactly the axis your safety-deliberation-marker count would need to keep watching even as `mu` varies.
- **grimjim, "Projected Abliteration" (Hugging Face blog).** **[LOW-CONFIDENCE, but directly useful engineering signal]**. Reports that **conventional (Arditi-style) abliteration produced flatly incoherent, ungrammatical output on Gemma 3 12B** without preprocessing, motivating a "projected abliteration" variant that decomposes the refusal direction into a component parallel to the harmless-mean direction (treated as a confound — general "helpfulness magnitude," not refusal-specific) and a component orthogonal to it, ablating only the orthogonal part. A commenter (p-e-w, the author of the Heretic tool) adds: **"newer dense models like Gemma 3 (and Qwen 3, Phi 4, and GPT-OSS) are substantially more resistant to abliteration,"** theorizing crude orthogonalization is more likely to cause damage on these specific families. Given our target family is Qwen (3.6, i.e. the direct successor to the Qwen 3 line named here), this is a concrete, actionable warning: **naive full-strength orthogonalization on Qwen may be more likely to break coherence than the Llama-2/Llama-3 results in the original Arditi paper would suggest.** This is exactly the kind of evidence that should push you toward (a) a conservative single-layer or narrow-layer-band ablation rather than all-layers, (b) validating coherence (not just refusal rate) at every `lambda`/`mu` setting, and (c) considering the projected/affine variants (§2a, this paragraph) instead of raw orthogonal projection if you see grammaticality loss.
- No paper we found directly tests "ablate a direction derived from a *different, more-aligned* checkpoint (base) applied to an *already less-aligned* target (TC), then merge with a *third, independently de-aligned* model (Fable)" — i.e., nobody has published the specific three-way stacking scenario your `W1`/`W2` design implies. Your `mu`-sweep governor is, as far as this search can tell, **a novel mitigation for a hypothesized-but-not-directly-documented failure mode**. That doesn't mean it's wrong — the Fafuła and grimjim evidence above is consistent with disposition/coherence drift under ablation being real and Qwen-family-sensitive — but you should treat "is W1 hypertrophied" as an empirical question your own pilot must answer, not something the literature has pre-answered either way.

## 3. Related direction-editing methods

- **LEACE — "LEAst-squares Concept Erasure,"** Belrose, Pope, Schoelkopf, Dupré, Biderman, arXiv:2306.03819 (2023). **[ESTABLISHED]**. Closed-form, provably-optimal **linear** concept erasure: an affine transform that guarantees zero linear separability of a target concept in a representation while provably minimizing the RMS distortion to everything else, among all transforms with that guarantee. Relevant to our work as the theoretical ceiling for "what's the least-damaging linear removal of a concept" — Arditi's orthogonal projection is a special, less-optimal case (it doesn't minimize collateral distortion the way LEACE's closed-form solution does). If coherence loss becomes a problem, a LEACE-style erasure (rather than raw `r r^T` projection) is a principled fallback with a formal minimal-distortion guarantee.
- **RepE — "Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency,"** Zou et al., arXiv:2310.01405 (2023). **[ESTABLISHED]**. The broader framework abliteration sits inside: read out and manipulate high-level concepts as directions in activation space, including safety-relevant ones. General finding: many "alignment-adjacent" behaviors (including refusal, honesty, power-seeking framing) are reasonably linearly represented and controllable this way, which is the foundational assumption Arditi et al.'s narrower refusal-specific result builds on.
- **Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)**, Rimsky et al., arXiv:2312.06681 (2023/2024). **[ESTABLISHED]**. Inference-time steering-vector addition (not weight editing) using the same difference-of-means idea; useful as the "non-destructive" sibling technique — if a `mu` setting of your hypertrophy governor is hard to get right as a permanent weight edit, an alternative is to keep `uTC` untouched and instead do inference-time steering at eval time to bracket the effect before committing to a weight-level merge.
- **Affine Concept Editing (ACE)**, arXiv:2411.09003 — see §2a. The most directly relevant refinement of Arditi's method for *avoiding* the coherence failure your `W2` governor is trying to head off from a different angle (ACE fixes it by keeping a reference offset; your governor fixes it by re-injecting a fraction of the aligned component post-hoc). These are not mutually exclusive — ACE could in principle be applied to compute `uTC` itself, as an alternative to plain orthogonal projection.
- **Checkpoint-audit / detectability**: "Has This Checkpoint Been Abliterated? A Two-Signal Audit and Its Failure Map," Hurtado, arXiv:2607.01854 (July 2026). **[EMERGING]**. Not directly about our method, but its two signals (activation refusal-gap ratio, and rank-1 spectral energy of the weight delta vs. a reference checkpoint) are a ready-made pair of **diagnostics you could reuse to quantify how "hard" your own ablation landed** — e.g., track the rank-1 spectral energy of `uTC - TC` across your `lambda`/layer choices as a cheap proxy for how large/concentrated the edit is, independent of running the full refusal probe.
- **Fine-tuning-based removal, as a comparison point**: "Badllama 3: removing safety fine-tuning from Llama 3 in minutes," arXiv:2407.01376. Shows LoRA/full fine-tuning can strip refusal even faster/cheaper than direction ablation — relevant mainly as context that Fable's own de-alignment (fine-tuning-based, not ablation-based) and your ablation-based `uTC` are two different *mechanisms* converging on similar behavior, which is exactly the premise behind worrying about "stacking" two independent de-alignment mechanisms in the hypertrophy hypothesis.

## 4. Practical parameter guidance for a 27B dense model

Synthesizing Arditi et al.'s original recipe with community-tool practice (Heretic — p-e-w's open-source abliteration tool, whose defaults are widely used and battle-tested across many recent releases) and the grimjim/Fafuła coherence-risk signals:

- **Prompt-set size**: 128 harmful + 128 harmless for direction extraction, 32+32 held out for layer/direction selection is the literature-anchored starting point (Arditi et al.). This was validated at 7B–70B; nothing suggests you need materially more at 27B, but given Qwen's documented resistance (§2c), consider a somewhat larger, more diverse harmful set (covering multiple refusal *categories* per §2b, not just one topic) to reduce the topic-overfitting risk flagged by the topic-matched-baseline critique below.
- **Layer sweep**: candidate layers across the full stack, but **exclude the top ~20% closest to unembedding** (`l < 0.8*L`, per Arditi et al.) — for a ~27B/likely 60+ layer dense model this still leaves a wide sweep window. Score each candidate on validation-set bypass-rate, induce-score sanity check, and **KL divergence on harmless prompts** as your coherence guard, not just the raw refusal-rate delta.
- **Target matrices**: `attn.o_proj` + `mlp.down_proj`, matching both the peer-reviewed original and the community-tool default (Heretic explicitly limits itself to these two matrix types per layer) — this validates the plan's stated targets directly.
- **Strength/scaling**: Heretic's practical lambda range is **0.8–1.5** (1.0 = the textbook Arditi projection), with an optional winsorization step (e.g. clip at the 95th percentile) to blunt outlier activations before computing the direction — worth adding if your GSM8K/probe pilot shows instability. There is no literature-derived "safe" lambda specific to 27B-Qwen; you should sweep it empirically the same way you're already planning to sweep `mu`.
- **Validation — what "good" vs. "bad" ablation looks like**: converging guidance across sources: (1) refusal-rate drop on a held-out harmful set (your 28-prompt probe's comply/hedge/refuse verdict), (2) **capability retention on a standard benchmark battery** — MMLU/GSM8K should stay within ~1–2 points, larger drift signals over-ablation (Arditi et al.'s own numbers); (3) **explicit coherence/grammaticality check** — read raw generations, don't just trust benchmark scores, because the RWKV and Gemma-3 incoherence failures (§2a, §2c) were not obviously visible in aggregate metrics until someone actually read the text; (4) optionally, the rank-1 spectral-energy-of-weight-delta diagnostic from the checkpoint-audit paper (§3) as a numeric "how big was this edit" sanity check across your lambda/mu grid, independent of any single benchmark.
- **Family-specific amber flag**: two independent signals (Arditi et al.'s own Qwen-7B outlier result, and the p-e-w/grimjim community observation naming Qwen 3 explicitly) both point at Qwen models being **more fragile to naive orthogonal-projection ablation** than the Llama family the technique was popularized on. Given Qwen3.6-27B is your exact base, budget real pilot time for a coherence check, and keep the ACE/projected-ablation variant (§2a/2c) as a documented fallback if plain orthogonal projection shows grammaticality loss at your chosen lambda.

## 5. Assessing the specific plan: base-derived `r-hat` transferred to TC

This is the load-bearing empirical question for the whole abliteration leg, and it is the one place the literature gives a reasonably direct, favorable-but-imperfect answer.

**Supporting evidence — cross-checkpoint transfer is measured, and looks good, in COSMIC.**
**"COSMIC: Generalized Refusal Direction Identification in LLM Activations,"** Siu, Crispino, Yu, Pan, Wang, Song, Wang, arXiv:2506.00085 / ACL Findings 2025. **[EMERGING, ACL-peer-reviewed]**. Two findings matter directly:
1. COSMIC replaces Arditi et al.'s output-behavior-based layer/direction selection with a **cosine-similarity-based, output-independent** selection criterion, which the authors show works even when the target model is **weakly aligned** (they explicitly test on `dolphin-2.9.4-llama3.1-8b` and `Llama-3.1-8B-Lexi-Uncensored-V2`, both already de-aligned fine-tunes) — this matters because Arditi et al.'s original *bypass_score/induce_score* selection criteria implicitly assume the target model still refuses robustly, which is precisely the assumption that breaks down for TC (only mildly aligned). If you use Arditi-style output-behavior selection to pick the best layer *on TC itself*, it may pick a poor layer because TC's own refusal signal is weak (exactly the concern the shared-context brief already flags) — COSMIC's activation-geometry-only selection criterion is a candidate fix if you need to select the layer using TC's own activations rather than transplanting base's chosen layer wholesale.
2. Cross-checkpoint transfer numbers **[secondary-synthesis, not directly verified against primary PDF text — the fetch tool could not extract this table directly; pulled via two independent search-engine syntheses that agreed]**: a direction fit on the **base** model transfers to **instruction-tuned** and **abliterated** variants with at most **~0.010 AUROC degradation in 3 of 4 model families tested**, with reported **base→instruct angular shifts of 17°–39°** specifically named for **Qwen2.5, Qwen3.5, and Llama-3.2**, and a cleaner **instruct→abliterated transfer** (11°–42° angular shift, AUROC degradation ≤0.003). If accurate, this is a striking, almost on-the-nose validation of exactly your proposed transfer direction (base→instruct-family checkpoint) for the Qwen lineage specifically. **Caveat**: I could not independently confirm this table by reading the primary PDF text (extraction failed twice); treat it as probably-real but not fully verified, and if this number matters to a go/no-go decision, pull the actual COSMIC paper table before relying on it.

**Supporting evidence — base models already encode refusal-adjacent circuitry.**
The general finding referenced in "Base LLMs refuse too" (community discussion, not a peer-reviewed claim I could verify at the paper level) and echoed in COSMIC's framing is that alignment fine-tuning (SFT/RLHF/DPO) is believed to *amplify pre-existing circuitry* in the base model rather than construct refusal from scratch. If true, it's a mechanistic reason to expect base-derived directions to generalize forward into aligned/lightly-aligned descendants of that same base — TC being exactly such a descendant. This is consistent with, but weaker evidence than, the COSMIC transfer numbers above.

**Caveats / pitfalls specific to your plan:**
1. **Fine-tuning can reorganize, not just rotate, refusal geometry.** "Dynamic Adversarial Fine-Tuning Reorganizes Refusal Geometry," Lan et al., arXiv:2604.27019. **[EMERGING]**. Under aggressive adversarial fine-tuning, the *best admissible carrier* (their term for the most effective ablation-relevant direction) **relocates across layers** during training — moving from late layers (layer 24) to early layers (layer 0) as training progressed — even while low-dimensional structure (effective rank ~1.2–1.3) persisted. Principal-angle drift was large (42–53°) even under their most refusal-robust training variant. This is a different fine-tuning regime than TC's concision-focused SFT (not adversarial red-teaming), so the magnitude may not transfer, but the *qualitative* lesson is directly relevant: **do not assume the layer that works best on base is still the best layer on TC.** Practically: re-validate (or re-sweep) the layer choice on TC's own activations rather than hard-coding the base-selected layer index, even if you extract the direction's *value* from base.
2. **TC's own refusal signal being weak is a feature for base-derived extraction, not just a caveat** — it's precisely why you're sourcing from base rather than TC in the first place, and COSMIC's result that Arditi-style *behavioral* layer-selection struggles on weakly-aligned models (point 1 above) directly supports this design choice, PROVIDED you don't also try to re-select the layer using TC's weak behavioral signal — use base's behavioral selection (base is robustly aligned) to pick the direction and layer, then just *apply* (don't re-select) on TC. That is what your plan already does; the literature supports doing it that way round rather than the reverse.
3. **Task-conditioned subspaces (§2b) mean a single base-derived direction may not uniformly ablate all refusal categories on TC**, even if the aggregate AUROC transfer looks clean. Your 28-prompt probe's discriminating meta-prompt tier is a reasonable single-axis pilot signal but isn't a full-coverage certification across categories — worth keeping the probe's category diversity in mind if you scale past the pilot.
4. **No literature we found tests the specific chain base→uTC→merge-with-independently-de-aligned-Fable→(optional)-mu-correction.** The COSMIC transfer evidence supports step one (direction generalizes base→TC-like checkpoint). Nothing supports or refutes what happens after that direction-ablated TC is merged with Fable — that is genuinely new empirical territory for your own pilot to characterize, consistent with what the shared context already calls "our craziness."

**Bottom line on the plan**: the base→TC direction-transfer premise is **better supported than we expected going in** — COSMIC's reported numbers, if they hold up on inspection, are close to a direct validation for the Qwen lineage specifically. The weaker link is not "does the direction transfer" but "does a single global direction, however well-transferred, uniformly ablate refusal across categories on TC, and does stacking it with Fable's independent de-alignment overshoot into the disposition-drift/coherence-loss territory documented (thinly) for Qwen in §2c." Your planned mu-sweep governor is a sound empirical answer to exactly that open question — the literature neither confirms nor refutes hypertrophy in your exact stacked configuration, so it remains something only your own pilot can settle, but it's a well-motivated hypothesis given the Qwen-specific fragility signals from two independent sources.
