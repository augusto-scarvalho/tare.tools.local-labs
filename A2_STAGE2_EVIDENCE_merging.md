## 1. Task arithmetic: the base layer

**Editing Models with Task Arithmetic**, Ilharco et al., ICLR 2023 — [arXiv:2212.04089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04089)

**[E]** Defines a task vector as τ = θ_ft − θ_pre (fine-tuned minus pretrained, same architecture/initialization). Three operations are validated empirically:
- **Addition**: θ_new = θ_pre + Σ τ_i improves multi-task performance simultaneously, without joint training.
- **Negation**: θ_new = θ_pre − τ *decreases* performance on the target task "with little change in model behavior on control tasks" — i.e., negation is reasonably targeted, not a blunt global degradation.
- **Analogies**: combining three task vectors (A, B, C) can improve a fourth target task with zero data from that task, when the tasks form an analogy relation (τ_D ≈ τ_B − τ_A + τ_C).

**[E]** All of this is defined relative to a **single shared pretrained checkpoint** — every task vector in the original paper is computed against the *same* θ_pre. This is directly relevant to your setup: TC−base and Fable−base (and by extension uTC−base) all share the identical base, so your merge sits inside the paper's *original, best-supported* regime, not in the harder "different-initialization" cross-model regime discussed in §3.

**[S]** The paper is light on precise coefficient-sweep theory. It reports that a single scaling coefficient α (applied uniformly to the summed task vector, tuned on a small held-out set) works well, but gives no closed-form account of *why* linearity holds or breaks down as α grows — later mechanistic work (below) fills this gap. Your own λ ∈ {0.4, 0.7, 1.0} sweep with monotonic concision and neutral-to-better accuracy at λ=1.0 is consistent with the paper's qualitative claim that moderate-to-full-strength addition of a well-behaved task vector doesn't require heavy damping — but the paper itself offers no guarantee this continues past λ=1.0, nor that it holds for a *modified* (abliterated) carrier as opposed to the original fine-tune's own vector.

## 2. Interference-aware merging: mechanisms, when they help, costs

### TIES-Merging — Yadav et al., NeurIPS 2023 — [arXiv:2306.01708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01708)

**[E]** Identifies two concrete interference sources in naive task-vector summation: (a) **redundancy** — many fine-tuned deltas are near-zero and just add noise; (b) **sign conflict** — different task vectors disagree on the sign of a given parameter, so summing partially cancels both. Fix = **Trim** (zero out low-magnitude entries per task vector, keeping only the top-k% by magnitude), **Elect Sign** (per-parameter, pick the sign with the greater total magnitude across tasks), **Merge** (average only the task vectors whose sign matches the elected sign). This consistently outperforms naive averaging/summation in multi-task settings.

**[S / relevant]**: TIES is designed for combining *several distinct, unrelated* task vectors into one multi-task model. It is not designed for your case — a single-target arithmetic edit (fable + λ·carrier) — but its diagnostic frame (redundancy + sign conflict) is exactly the right lens for evaluating whether uTC−base and Fable's own implicit de-alignment component conflict or reinforce (§4).

### DARE — "Language Models are Super Mario," Yu et al. — [arXiv:2311.03099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03099)

**[E]** DARE randomly zeroes a fraction *p* of delta parameters and rescales survivors by 1/(1−p). Empirically: up to ~90% of delta parameters can be dropped with negligible loss; at 70B scale up to ~99%; **drop-rate tolerance scales with model size** (WizardMath-70B survives p=0.99; the 7B/13B variants fail at that rate). DARE composes with Task Arithmetic and TIES as a pre-sparsification step, and the paper reports TIES benefits *more* from DARE pre-sparsification than plain task arithmetic does.

**[E] Failure conditions (important for a 27B, fp16→Q4 pipeline)**: DARE only works when the **delta parameter magnitudes are small** (paper's working range: |δ| ≲ 0.002). When base-vs-fine-tune magnitude is large (mismatched backbones, or continued pretraining pushing δ up toward ~0.03), performance collapses catastrophically (one reported case: 63.41/55.4 → 0.0/0.0 accuracy after removing just 10% of deltas). Dropping the *fine-tuned weights themselves* instead of the delta is also catastrophic (67.20 → 8.56 AlpacaEval at p=0.1) — rescaling must operate on the delta, not the absolute weight.

**Relevance to you**: you're not using DARE, but this is the strongest available evidence on *when delta-based edits are robust to further perturbation* — a useful sanity check before quantizing an abliterated delta (§6): if your uTC−base or CA deltas are unusually large-magnitude compared to typical SFT deltas, expect fragility under further compression.

### Model soups — Wortsman et al., ICML 2022 — [arXiv:2203.05482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05482)

**[E]** Simple weight averaging of models fine-tuned from the *same* pretrained init with different hyperparameters improves accuracy/robustness over any single member, at zero inference cost, because such models tend to lie in the same low-loss basin (linear mode connectivity, §3). This is the "zero-order" precedent for task arithmetic — it establishes that same-basin weight-space linear combination is often safe, which is the load-bearing assumption under your entire Stage-2 plan.

### SLERP

**[E, but note: no peer-reviewed LLM-specific paper]** Spherical linear interpolation treats two weight vectors as points on a hypersphere and interpolates along the great-circle path rather than the straight chord, preserving norm and avoiding the "regression to the mean" that plain linear averaging causes in high dimensions. Standard in practitioner tooling (mergekit) but the evidence base is empirical/community (Labonne et al. blog + mergekit repo), not a peer-reviewed arXiv result — treat claims of SLERP superiority as **[S]** relative to task arithmetic / TIES.

### Fisher-weighted merging — Matena & Raffel, NeurIPS 2022 — [arXiv:2111.09832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09832)

**[E]** Frames merging as approximate Bayesian posterior fusion: plain averaging = isotropic-Gaussian posterior approximation; Fisher merging weights each parameter by its Fisher information (curvature/importance), significantly outperforming isotropic averaging and approaching the cost of full ensembling at a fraction of the inference cost. **[S, applicable to you]**: this suggests a principled refinement over your flat-λ scalar — importance-weighting the (uTC−base) carrier per-parameter (or per-layer) by Fisher information computed on a concision-relevant dataset could, in principle, let you push λ higher in low-curvature (safe) regions while damping it in high-curvature (fragile) ones. Nobody has published this specific combination (task-vector carrier × Fisher weighting × abliteration) — this is a plausible but untested extension, not an established technique.

### Task Singular Vectors / Spectral Over-Accumulation (directly bears on your "hypertrophy" question)

- **Task Singular Vectors**, Gargiulo et al. — [arXiv:2412.00081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00081): reframes interference at the level of the *singular value decomposition* of each per-layer task matrix rather than raw parameter signs. Overlapping singular vectors across tasks ("shared features in weight space") introduce interference when merged, degrading individual-task performance.
- **Spectral Over-Accumulation in Model Merging** — [arXiv:2602.05536](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05536v1) (Singular Value Calibration, SVC): **[E]** *"When several task matrices align in the same spectral subspaces, a simple linear combination repeatedly aggregates the same shared spectral components, leading to spectral over-counting in those subspaces,"* and *"positive cross-task overlap inflates the merged singular value above the projection-optimal magnitude."* The proposed fix rescales each subspace's singular value by a calibration factor γ = K / Σᵢ max(α, sᵢ), where sᵢ are per-task projection/overlap coefficients and **α ∈ (0,1] is a hyperparameter that caps the correction, acting as a governor: α=1 gives "suppression-only" behavior (γ≤1, i.e. can only shrink, never amplify, an over-represented subspace)**.

**This is the single most directly relevant precedent for your μ·CA governor.** It is *documented, quantified, peer-reviewed evidence* that: (1) additive combination of task vectors that share a directional component over-amplifies that component relative to what either task vector alone "intended," and (2) the standard fix is a calibration/damping coefficient applied specifically to the shared/over-represented subspace, not a blanket rescale of the whole merge. This validates the *shape* of your W2 = W1 − μ·CA proposal (isolate the shared direction, then apply a tunable corrective coefficient) far better than it validates your *specific* isolation method — SVC identifies the shared subspace via SVD overlap of the actual task matrices being merged, whereas CA = base_ablit − base is a direction estimated from a *different* pair (base vs. its own abliteration) and merely assumed to be "the same" de-alignment direction that Fable's own fine-tune and uTC's abliteration both contain. That assumption is the crux — see §4.

## 3. Cross-model transfer, weight disentanglement, and when linearity breaks

**[E]** "Weight disentanglement" (formalized across several 2024–2026 papers, e.g. [arXiv:2407.07089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07089), [arXiv:2604.17078](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17078v1), [arXiv:2511.14569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14569)) is the property that lets task-vector addition work at all: each task vector should act mostly within its own local region of input/representation space, so that adding two task vectors doesn't corrupt either task's function individually. This property is *not automatic* — it correlates with (a) how far fine-tuning moved from the pretrained checkpoint (tangent-space / NTK-linear regime — small moves disentangle better), (b) which modules were fine-tuned (attention-only fine-tunes disentangle better than full fine-tunes per 2407.07089), and (c) task similarity (more similar tasks interfere more, ironically, because their local domains overlap).

**[E]** "Task Arithmetic in the Tangent Space" (NeurIPS 2023, Ortiz-Jimenez et al.) shows that linearizing the model (first-order Taylor expansion around θ_pre — i.e., treating fine-tuning as if it were a linear operation in weight-space, an NTK approximation) *improves* weight disentanglement and reduces task interference relative to standard nonlinear fine-tuning. The practical takeaway: **task arithmetic's linear-addition assumption is a better approximation the smaller/more local the fine-tuning delta is**, and degrades as fine-tunes move the model further from θ_pre in ways that engage genuinely nonlinear behavior. TC, Fable, and your abliteration edit are all *substantial* interventions on a 27B model (SFT-scale fine-tunes, not LoRA-scale), so you should not assume near-perfect linearity — this is consistent with your own Stage-1 result (λ=1.0 wasn't degenerate, but you saw *monotonic*, not flat, movement, i.e., real but bounded nonlinearity).

**[S — genuinely open, and the closest literature to your exact "carrier transfer" question]**: A small but growing 2025–2026 line explicitly studies transferring a task vector *computed on one model* into a *different* model:
- **Cross-Model Transfer of Task Vectors via Few-Shot Orthogonal Alignment** — [arXiv:2505.12021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12021): shows that naive direct application of a task vector across models (even same-architecture, different init/fine-tune lineage) underperforms, and that you generally need a learned alignment transform (orthogonal Procrustes-style, estimated from a handful of labeled examples) before the transplant works well. Direct transplantation without alignment is presented as the *weak baseline*, not the method being validated.
- **Bilinear Coordinate Alignment for Training-Free Task-Vector Transfer** — [arXiv:2605.28444](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.28444): same problem, different (bilinear, training-free) alignment map; again the framing is "raw transfer needs correction."

**Important scoping note**: both of these papers target the *harder* problem — transferring a capability delta across genuinely different pretrained models/initializations, where the two "coordinate systems" in weight space are not aligned at all. **Your case is easier**: uTC, Fable, and base are the *same* architecture *and* the same pretrained initialization; you're not doing cross-initialization transfer, you're doing same-basis task-vector addition with a *modified* (abliterated) version of one of the vectors. The formally closer question is: "is (uTC − base) still a valid task vector in the Ilharco sense, i.e., does it still point toward the same local capability region, after you've surgically edited a subspace of TC's weights?" No paper in this search directly answers that. It is a genuinely novel-enough combination (arithmetic merge of a mechanistically-edited fine-tune) that your planned empirical validation (concision pilot + discriminating-tier refusal check before a full pass) is doing real evidentiary work the literature can't currently substitute for.

## 4. The "carrier" (uTC) and "governor" (−μ·CA) ideas specifically

### Abliteration mechanics — what's established

**[E]** Refusal-direction ablation: [Arditi et al., "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction," arXiv:2406.11717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717) — across 13 open chat models up to 72B, a *single* difference-of-means direction (harmful-minus-harmless prompt activations) mediates refusal: erasing it from the residual stream at all layers/positions suppresses refusal; adding it induces refusal even on harmless prompts. This is the mechanism your Stage-2 plan (compute r-hat from base, orthogonalize o_proj/down_proj) directly implements — it is the standard, well-replicated method (matches the "Heretic"/community abliteration tooling ecosystem too).

**[E, complicating]** [Marshall, Scherlis & Belrose, "Refusal in LLMs is an Affine Function," arXiv:2411.09003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09003) extends the single-direction picture to an *affine* one (direction + bias/offset term), and shows their Affine Concept Editing gives more precise, more reliable refusal control than pure directional ablation across ten models — implying pure linear-subspace orthogonalization (what you're planning) is a good first-order approximation but leaves some controllable residual on the table.

**[E, directly complicating your CA assumption]**: Multiple 2025–2026 papers push back on the strict "refusal = one direction" simplification your CA construction relies on:
- **"Safety Subspaces are Not Linearly Distinct: A Fine-Tuning Case Study"** — [arXiv:2505.14185](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14185): tested across five Llama/Qwen-family models. Core finding: *"subspaces that amplify safe behaviors also amplify useful ones, and prompts with different safety implications activate overlapping representations"* — i.e., **safety is entangled with general capability, not a clean orthogonal subspace**. This directly bears on whether CA = base_ablit − base is a "pure" de-alignment vector as your plan assumes, or whether it also drags along general-capability changes that would then get subtracted out too when you apply −μ·CA.
- **"Over-Refusal and Representation Subspaces"** — [arXiv:2603.27518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27518): the harmful-refusal direction itself is reasonably task-agnostic/global (consistent with Arditi), but *related* over-refusal / hedging behavior spans a **higher-dimensional, task-dependent subspace** not captured by a single global direction. Relevant because your alignment metric (safety-deliberation-marker count, "balk" rate) is measuring exactly this broader hedging behavior, not just hard refusal — a single-direction r-hat may under-address it.
- **"Abliteration Is Not a Scalpel"** — [arXiv:2607.17427](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17427): finds abliteration reliably suppresses *expressed* refusal/hedging language while leaving underlying decision *behavior* mostly unchanged in some dimensions (instruction-following stayed at 100% JSON validity; general capability covariates were unaffected) but produces family-specific, sometimes divergent shifts in disposition (e.g., opposite confidence-direction changes between Gemma and Qwen families after the "same" intervention). Practical implication for you: abliteration's off-target effects are **not uniform across architectures**, so results from published abliteration studies on Llama/Gemma/Qwen-8B-class models should not be assumed to transfer cleanly to your Qwen3.6-27B dense base without your own validation (which your plan already provides for via the discriminating-tier check — good).
- **Comparative abliteration methods across architectures** — [arXiv:2512.13655](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.13655): benchmarks several concrete abliteration implementations (Heretic, JIM-plus, ErisForge, "Projected Abliteration") on capability retention (MMLU/GSM8K/HellaSwag) alongside safety-removal efficacy; the general framing across this literature is that **layer and direction selection matter a lot** (not all layers/directions are equally good ablation targets) — supporting your plan's step of "pick best layer" empirically rather than ablating uniformly by default.

### Does W1 double-count de-alignment? Is the −μ·CA governor logic sound or flawed?

Putting the pieces together:

**[S, on balance supportive of your instinct, evidence is indirect not direct]**
1. Fable is *already* an uncensored fine-tune of base — i.e., Fable's own weights already encode *some* de-alignment displacement relative to base, in whatever direction/subspace DavidAU's training moved it. Call that (implicit, un-isolated) displacement δ_Fable.
2. uTC − base = (TC − base) − r-hat(r-hat·TC) is *not* a pure concision vector; it's concision **plus** an abliteration edit that removes TC's own (mild) alignment. So it, too, carries a de-alignment component, roughly parallel to r-hat.
3. If δ_Fable and r-hat (or its projection into uTC−base) point in a **similar** direction in weight space — plausible, since "de-alignment" plausibly funnels through a low-dimensional, largely shared subspace per Arditi's single-direction finding, even though 2505.14185/2603.27518 complicate the "single" part — then W1 = fable + λ(uTC − base) is summing two task-vector components that overlap in that subspace. That is *exactly* the geometry that "Spectral Over-Accumulation" (§2, arXiv:2602.05536) and "Task Singular Vectors" (arXiv:2412.00081) show causes over-amplification (their "double scaling when aligned" / "spectral over-counting" result) rather than clean additive composition.
4. **However**, your own Stage-1 data is the strongest available evidence and it currently argues *against* hypertrophy being a problem at λ=1.0 with the *unablated* carrier: l1.0 already matched fable-plain on both balk rate (1/8) and deliberation-marker count (0), i.e., no *observable* over-de-alignment signature at full strength with TC's own (non-abliterated) mild alignment vector. That's evidence the *un-abliterated* system isn't hypertrophied. It does **not** directly tell you whether swapping in uTC (with its stronger, surgically-injected de-alignment component replacing TC's weak native one) changes that balance — uTC's de-alignment push is engineered to be much larger/cleaner than TC's incidental one, so the "how much overlap accumulates" premise changes with the swap. This is precisely why your plan's structure (test W1 first, only reach for −μ·CA if W1 shows evidence of over-de-alignment — excessive compliance on things that should still get hedged, degeneration, or loss of nuance — rather than assuming hypertrophy a priori) is the methodologically correct order of operations given the literature: **the governor is a plausible, well-precedented *response* to a measured symptom, not something to apply prophylactically.** SVC (§2) only applies its correction where it *measures* positive overlap (its α parameter is explicitly a "suppression-only, never amplify" cap) — it doesn't assume overlap and subtract by default. Mirror that: measure whether W1 over-shoots toward compliance/incoherence relative to Fable-plain and TC/uTC-implied targets, and only then sweep μ.

5. **A distinct, more specific literature-supported reason to expect trouble** independent of the "hypertrophy" framing: the safety/utility-conflict merging literature (LED-Merging [arXiv:2502.16770](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16770), SafeMERGE [arXiv:2503.17239](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17239)) treats safety-vs-utility merging conflicts as *real and directionally asymmetric* — SafeMERGE's mechanism is a per-layer cosine-similarity threshold τ deciding whether to merge a fine-tuned layer at all, precisely because *some* layers' fine-tuned deltas conflict with the safety-relevant direction and merging them regardless silently erodes alignment (their problem is the mirror image of yours — they're trying to *preserve* alignment while merging in capability; you're trying to *preserve* a de-alignment result while merging in concision — but the mechanism, per-layer/per-direction conflict requiring a tunable gate rather than uniform λ, is the same one that would justify a −μ·CA-style correction if you find W1 needs it).
6. Also relevant: **"When Safe Models Merge into Danger"** [arXiv:2604.00627](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.00627) shows merging can produce emergent behavior *neither* parent model has (their case: latent unsafe capability appearing from two safe parents), which is the general warning that "the merge's alignment character is not simply interpolated between its parents' — it can be nonlinear and must be measured, not assumed." This applies in either direction (could make W1 more compliant than either Fable or uTC alone, or in principle less) and is the strongest general argument for keeping your empirical alignment probe as the arbiter rather than trusting the arithmetic reasoning alone — which is already your plan's design.

**Bottom line on the governor question**: the −μ*CA idea is **not flawed in structure** — it has a genuine, peer-reviewed mechanistic analogue (spectral/subspace over-accumulation correction, safety-utility merge gating) — but **its necessity is an empirical question your Stage-1 result doesn't yet answer for the abliterated carrier**, and its *isolation* method (CA computed from base vs. base_ablit, assumed transferable to the Fable/uTC merge) is the weakest link per the safety-subspace-entanglement evidence (§4, arXiv:2505.14185): CA may not be a "pure" de-alignment direction, so subtracting μ·CA risks removing some general capability along with alignment, at any μ>0. Recommend validating CA's purity cheaply before sweeping μ: check CA's effect on a capability-only probe (e.g., held-out MMLU-style QA or your GSM8K set) at a couple of μ values, to see whether −μ·CA measurably taxes plain capability, not just compliance.

## 6. Practical guidance

**Coefficient ranges.**
- **[E]** Task-arithmetic α: literature typically grid-searches on a small held-out set rather than asserting a universal value; single-task addition often works well at α≈1.0 for well-behaved task vectors, but multi-task sums frequently need α<1 (0.3–0.8 range common in TIES/DARE papers) to avoid interference as more vectors stack. Your single-carrier λ∈{0.4,0.7,1.0} sweep is well within the range the literature treats as safe to explore, and your finding that λ=1.0 was still improving (monotonic, no degeneration) rather than already past a peak is a reasonable, literature-consistent signal to also test λ>1.0 if you want a ceiling — though note DARE/TIES evidence that higher coefficients amplify interference risk once you complicate the carrier (uTC vs TC).
- **[E]** Safety-utility merge gates (SafeMERGE's τ, general "linear merge weight" surveys) most often land in ranges where the safety-preserving component keeps a nontrivial floor rather than being driven near zero — the closest numeric anchor found here is a report that best linear-merge results (unrelated capability-merging context, not abliteration-specific) cluster around weight splits of 0.9/0.1 to 0.6/0.4 rather than symmetric 0.5/0.5 or near-0/1 splits. Treat μ∈[0,~0.6] as the more literature-plausible sweep range before μ→1 (full subtraction), consistent with your plan's mu∈[0,1] framing; don't expect a linear μ→outcome relationship given the entanglement evidence in §4.

**Quantization interactions.**
- **[E]** [Task Vector Quantization, arXiv:2503.06921](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.06921v1) shows task-vector deltas can be quantized to ≤4 bits within merging pipelines with a residual/offset decomposition (base + offset), specifically because delta magnitudes are typically much smaller and more structured than the full weights — but the paper's method is a *deliberate* quantization scheme for the delta (decomposed, bit-allocated by sensitivity), not "quantize the final merged fp16 weights with off-the-shelf Q4_K_M and hope the delta survives." **[S, your actual pipeline]**: your process (orthogonalize in fp16 → merge in fp16 → quantize the *final* merged weights to Q4_K_M) means the abliteration edit and the λ·(uTC−base) carrier both get folded into the full-precision weight before quantization noise is applied — this is safer than quantizing the delta in isolation, but a genuinely small, low-magnitude direction (a single orthogonalized subspace, or a small-μ·CA correction) risks being partially within the rounding noise floor of Q4_K_M block quantization, especially in layers where the base weight magnitude is large relative to the edit. Recommend a direct check: compare the *effect size* of your alignment probe (balk rate, deliberation markers) between the fp16 and Q4_K_M version of the same merge (you may already be doing this per "quantized Q4_K_M matched" in Stage-1) — if Q4 washes out a small correction, μ sweeps done only in fp16 could mislead you about what ships.
- **[E]** DARE's failure-mode evidence (§2) — that small-magnitude deltas (|δ|≲0.002) survive aggressive perturbation but larger ones catastrophically don't — is a generically useful sanity bound: if you can cheaply measure ||uTC−base|| and ||CA|| per-layer, layers where these are unusually large relative to typical SFT-delta magnitudes are the ones most likely to misbehave under quantization and are worth spot-checking first.

**Degeneration / quality validation.**
- **[E]** No single automatic metric suffices; the standard combination in the neural-text-degeneration literature is **distinct-n / diversity** (n-gram repetition rate — catches repetition loops) + **MAUVE or perplexity-based coherence** (catches fluency collapse) + a semantic/task metric (your GSM8K accuracy already covers this for the concision axis). Caveat: **[E]** distinct-n alone is gameable (even random text scores well) and low perplexity alone drives *toward* degeneration (bland, repetitive, "safe" text can have very low perplexity) — so don't use perplexity as a standalone quality gate, and don't rely on distinct-n without a companion coherence check.
- **[S, applied to you]**: for W1/W2 candidates specifically, a cheap degeneration triage before a full pass would be: run your existing 28-prompt alignment probe's creative-writing subset, compute distinct-2/distinct-3 on the completions, and eyeball for repetition loops — this is a ~5-minute addition to your existing discriminating-tier check, reusing text you're already generating, and would catch a "hypertrophy manifesting as degeneration rather than as compliance-shift" failure mode that your current balk-rate/deliberation-marker metrics wouldn't directly flag.
